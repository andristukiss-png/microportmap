Research standards

Verification guide

A project appearing on MicroportMap means that a source reported it. It does not automatically prove the site is operating today, publicly accessible, or approved for flights.

Step 1

Find the source

Open the primary cited report and check publisher, publication date, geography, and facility identity. Favor first-party announcements and official records, while noting their limitations.

Step 2

Separate claims from observations

Distinguish proposed, pilot, installed and operational claims. A product announcement or demonstration is not proof of continuous public operations.

Step 3

Confirm location precision

Some map markers are approximate city or district representations rather than real landing coordinates. Never use map points for navigation or flight planning.

Step 4

Check status and freshness

Compare reports with more recent official notices. Mark conflicting or undated claims for editorial review rather than silently treating them as verified.

Editorial review checklist

  • Identify the named infrastructure project and reported operator.
  • Record a working source URL and report date where available.
  • State the type of infrastructure and degree of location precision.
  • Keep any uncertainty visible in notes rather than inventing exact coordinates.
  • Do not describe a facility as an open or approved landing site without evidence.
  • Never infer an affiliation between map projects and DRONairport.
