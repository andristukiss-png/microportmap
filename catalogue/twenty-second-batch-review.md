# Twenty-second sourced expansion: South Korean flight-test centres

Reviewed 30 October 2026. Six individually named and located centres bring the catalogue to 316 records across 40 countries, leaving 684 toward 1,000. All prior 310 records remain unchanged. South Korea is a new catalogue country. These are source-reported testing facilities, not independently verified commercial delivery ports.

## Evidence and counting decisions

Each centre has its own official Drone Information Portal facility page. KIAST’s current facility directory independently lists the six names and street addresses within the same institutional source family; this is a cross-check, not independent operational verification. Older English project descriptions still refer to proposed construction and unnamed additions; the current named pages, rather than that project total, determine this batch.

- Yeongwol: one centre; environmental chambers and thrust equipment are components.
- Goseong: the Donghae-myeon facility is in South Gyeongsang, not the northern county of the same name.
- Boeun: its ground testing and outdoor flight area are one centre.
- Hwaseong: two helipads do not create two extra sites.
- Incheon: both sources identify 194-1 Jeongseojin-ro despite differing district labels. Use the stable city/street identity. The Robot Land headquarters and separate certification facilities are not counted as this centre.
- Uiseong: the address is corroborated by KIAST’s directory. A reused phone number/image caption in the portal is not used to identify the location. Landing pads and additional test areas remain components of one facility.

Source pages are undated, so publication dates remain null. AIP amendment labels, copyright years and crawl dates are not publication dates. No aviation coordinates, operating windows, technical test parameters or source airspace polygons are repurposed as location or permission guidance. Map points are deliberately broad editorial locality references, not exact facility or landing coordinates.
